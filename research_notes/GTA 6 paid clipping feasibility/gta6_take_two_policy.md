## 2. Rockstar / Take-Two official policy on posting and monetising gameplay footage

### Takeaway
The governing document is Rockstar Support's "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material" (last updated 10 January 2025; not updated for or naming GTA 6). It tolerates non-commercial gameplay uploads, defines non-commercial as not making money from the footage, and flags cutscene/ending/spoiler compilations, isolated in-game TV/comedy and any pre-release footage for removal. Ad-monetised gameplay is tolerated in practice but not licensed in writing, and Take-Two reserves the right to take anything down at any time.

### Cited Findings
- The article is titled "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material", last updated 10 January 2025; it does not name GTA 6. — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material); date and GTA 6 omission relayed by [prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/blog/gta-6-youtube-copyright-monetization)
- General stance (quoted): "Generally, Take-Two Interactive does not object to our fans using materials for non-commercial uses in a manner which does not intentionally spoil the plot for others." — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Gameplay (quoted): "We're happy for fans to upload footage of their gameplay". — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Non-commercial definition (quoted): "'Non-commercial' means that you don't make money through the game footage you post or use the material as part of a promotion for a product or service." — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/en-US/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz)
- Spoilers (quoted): "Posting video showing the ending of the game, an edited combination of cutscenes and/or in-game plot dialogue, or any other big reveal in the story. We prefer fans to experience these in the context of the game's world, so any posting of these in isolation will be flagged for removal." — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- In-game TV shows and comedy performances posted in isolation will be flagged for removal. — [Rockstar Support (legacy URL)](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/200153756/Policy-on-posting-copyrighted-Rockstar-Games-material)
- Pre-release (quoted): "Any posting of in-game footage from leaked copies of the game prior to its official release date will be taken down", regardless of how the game was obtained. — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Scope (quoted): "this policy is intended to address occasional, non-commercial in-game content use by individual members of the Rockstar fan community." — relayed by [openworlddaily](https://openworlddaily.com/guides/gta-6-footage-streaming-policy/)
- Reservation (quoted): "Take-Two Interactive reserves the right to have its copyrighted material taken down at any time." Questions go to copyright@take2games.com. — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)
- Monetisation: secondary analyses say the policy lists six takedown categories and "Monetization is not one of the six", that no sentence explicitly allows monetisation via YouTube ads or Twitch subs, and that "monetized and sponsored videos rely on Rockstar's tolerance rather than a licence." — [bo3.gg](https://bo3.gg/games/articles/gta-6-content-creator-rules-spoilers-streaming-and-youtube-monetization); [prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/blog/gta-6-youtube-copyright-monetization); [nano-crea](https://nano-crea.com/gta6/guides/rockstar-rules)
- The spoiler rule has no end date: Insider Gaming noted nothing in the policy says when spoiler content becomes "safe"; the "even after launch" framing is the outlet's reading, not Rockstar wording. The policy does not use the words DMCA or strikes. — [planetgta6](https://www.planetgta6.com/en/news/gta-6-rockstar-spoiler-policy-raises-dmca-risk/); [bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/articles/gta-6-spoiler-video-rules)
- The policy's substance dates to GTA V launch day (17 September 2013), when Rockstar published the same "share, don't spoil" rules; Techdirt characterised it as Rockstar promising to use copyright to delete spoiler cutscene videos. — [GameSpot, Sept 2013](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/rockstar-outlines-user-upload-policy-on-gtav-launch-day/1100-6414525/); [Game Informer, 16 Sept 2013](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2013/09/16/rockstar-details-allowable-content-and-uses-of-game-footage.aspx); [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/tag/cutscenes/)
- A separate Rockstar Support article (last updated 3 April 2024) covers only GTA V radio tracks in Rockstar Editor videos shared to YouTube ("Information About Being Safe From Copyright Strikes When Uploading Rockstar Editor Videos"). — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/3f8XH01Kodw4zKnwbF6yTB/information-about-being-safe-from-copyright-strikes-when-uploading-rockstar-editor-videos)
- A Canadian law-firm note discusses GTA 6 creator, brand and RP-server exposure under this policy (confirms that brand/promotional use is outside the fan-use tolerance). — [For Founders Law](https://www.forfounderslaw.com/insights/gta-6-creators-brands-canada/)

### Inferences
- A paid clipping operation is squarely "commercial" under Rockstar's own definition in two ways: clippers are paid per view for footage, and clips promote a streamer/brand ("use the material as part of a promotion for a product or service"). It is not covered by the written tolerance. In practice Rockstar has tolerated a decade of monetised GTA V let's-plays, streams and clip channels, so the realistic risk is selective takedown, not blanket enforcement, but there is no licence to point to.
- The content types most likely to be removed in launch week are exactly the ones that perform best as short clips: endings, major story reveals, stitched cutscenes and isolated in-game TV/comedy bits. Clips of emergent gameplay with streamer reaction on screen fit the tolerated pattern best.
- Because the policy says spoilers posted "in isolation" are flagged, a streamer-reaction overlay over a cutscene may reduce but does not remove risk; the trigger is the story content, not the absence of commentary.

### Gaps
- Could not open the primary article, so could not confirm whether the current text contains any explicit sentence on YouTube Partner Programme monetisation, or the exact list of six categories. Secondary sources agree it does not explicitly authorise ads.
- Take-Two's general Terms of Service / EULA language on user-generated content and streaming was not retrieved.
- No GTA 6-specific update to the policy was found as of 17 November 2026; one could appear on launch day as happened with GTA V in 2013.

## 3. Take-Two enforcement history (2021-2026) and what it implies for launch week

### Takeaway
Take-Two is among the most aggressive publishers on leaks and unofficial reproductions: mass takedowns after the September 2022 leak, strikes on trailer re-uploads, mod takedowns, and in August 2026 same-day mass DMCA notices plus federal DMCA subpoenas to Discord, Microsoft/GitHub, X and Google to unmask leakers. Enforcement is fast and broad with collateral damage, yet re-uploads still reached millions of views.

### Cited Findings
- September 2022: after 90+ videos and screenshots of an early GTA 6 build leaked, Take-Two issued takedowns from a Rockstar-affiliated address across YouTube, Twitter and other sites; press read the strikes as confirmation of authenticity. — [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/take-two-strikes-gta-6-leaked-footage-seemingly-confirming-legitimacy); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/96015-take-two-floods-internet-copyright-strikes-following-gta.html)

### Inferences
- Take-Two files manual DMCA notices (which produce strikes on YouTube and account locks on X), not just Content ID claims. A clip account network that catches strikes loses accounts, not just revenue; three strikes terminates a YouTube channel.
- Enforcement priority order, judging by history: (1) pre-release/leaked footage, (2) wholesale re-uploads of official trailers, (3) story-spoiler/cutscene compilations, (4) mods/recreations. Ordinary post-release gameplay with commentary has not historically been targeted at scale.
- The August 2026 episode shows Take-Two's vendors over-match (official screenshots and journalism were struck). During launch week, expect false positives on legitimate clips, with slow appeals at exactly the moment views matter.
- The willingness to subpoena platforms for uploader identities raises the stakes for anyone posting footage obtained before street date.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on how many gameplay-with-commentary videos Take-Two has struck post-release (GTA V, RDR2); evidence is anecdotal.
- No documented cases found of Take-Two striking streamer clip channels specifically.
- Sources on the August 2026 leak include mirror/aggregator sites; TorrentFreak and VGC are the reliable ones.

## 4. Licensed music risk (radio stations, Content ID, mutes) and streamer mode

### Takeaway
Rockstar's footage tolerance does not extend to the third-party licensed music on GTA radio; labels' Content ID and Twitch audio recognition act independently. No streamer mode or licensed-music toggle has been officially announced for GTA 6 (GTA V and RDR2 never had one); a second-hand report of a music-filter mode exists but is unconfirmed. Radio audio in clips is the most likely cause of claims, mutes and lost campaign payouts.

### Cited Findings
- Rockstar has not announced a streamer mode or licensed-music toggle for GTA 6; GTA 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2 had none; Cyberpunk 2077 is cited as a game whose streamer mode disables a small set of tracks. — [Eklipse blog](https://blog.eklipse.gg/streaming-tips/gta-6-music-radio.html)
- RUMOUR: on 2 September 2026 the fan site WikiGTAVI reported that an unnamed creator who visited Rockstar was shown a mode that filters copyrighted music ("they showed me that, it works"); WikiGTAVI itself called it "not a promise on a feature list". A targeted follow-up search could not find the original report. — [mneemo](https://www.mneemo.com/blog/gta-6-streaming-music-copyright-twitch-youtube/); [bo3.gg](https://bo3.gg/games/articles/gta-6-content-creator-rules-spoilers-streaming-and-youtube-monetization)
- Rockstar has previewed six radio stations (Cocoteo FM, Back Country Radio, AfroBank FM, The Chamber 106.6, Flash FM, Dirty South Classics); full tracklists due at launch; characters can play radio, music and podcasts through their phones while on foot. — [Eklipse blog](https://blog.eklipse.gg/streaming-tips/gta-6-music-radio.html); [iTechGuides](https://www.itechguides.com/gta-vi-music-what-creators-can-safely-use-in-videos-and-streams)
- Rockstar's published guidance "does not establish a blanket GTA VI soundtrack license for creators"; YouTube and Twitch apply their own music-rights rules. The only Rockstar music guidance is the 3 April 2024 article on GTA V radio tracks in Rockstar Editor videos on YouTube, under which tracks bring no copyright strike but artists/labels can still monetise or block the video. — [iTechGuides](https://www.itechguides.com/gta-vi-music-what-creators-can-safely-use-in-videos-and-streams); [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/3f8XH01Kodw4zKnwbF6yTB/information-about-being-safe-from-copyright-strikes-when-uploading-rockstar-editor-videos)
- YouTube: a song match through Content ID produces a claim (not automatically a strike) that can redirect revenue or block the video; Content ID claims on radio songs can still happen in GTA 6 gameplay. — [Mubert](https://mubert.com/blog/gta-6-dmca-radio-streaming-content-id); [thumblore](https://www.thumblore.com/blogs/gta-6-youtube-monetization-rules)
- Twitch: audio recognition can mute VOD sections and delete Clips containing detected music; a muted section loses all audio including commentary; Twitch has no YouTube-style claim-and-monetise system. Twitch's November 2020 post documents the mass music DMCA wave. — [Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/twitch-copyright-rules-in-music-clips-and-vods); [Twitch blog, 11 Nov 2020](https://blog.twitch.com/en/2020/11/11/music-related-copyright-claims-and-twitch/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/twitch-hit-with-another-wave-of-dmca-takedowns)
- A publisher's streaming permission may not extend to third-party music inside the game; composition and sound-recording rights may need separate clearance. — [Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/twitch-copyright-claims-strikes-and-safe-practices); [Bristows](https://www.bristows.com/news/video-game-streaming)
- GamingBible (17 October 2025) ran a piece on GTA 6's "Twitch problem" (licensed music and DMCA for streamers); page could not be read. — [GamingBible](https://www.gamingbible.com/news/gta-6-twitch-problem-467339-20251017)
- Paid clipping campaigns pay on approved/verified views and payouts can be zero if a clip is not approved. — [gta6clippers.com](https://gta6clippers.com/learn/what-clippers-get-paid)

### Inferences
- A clip with radio audible is exposed on every platform: YouTube Shorts (Content ID claim removes it from the Shorts revenue pool for the creator or blocks it in some territories), TikTok/Instagram (audio muted or video removed), Twitch (clip deleted). For a view-based payout campaign, a muted or blocked clip earns nothing, so music is a direct payout risk, not only a copyright risk.
- Until a streamer mode is confirmed in the shipped game's settings, the only safe workflow is source streams with radio off, or clips cut from on-foot/mission segments, or audio replaced in the edit. Big streamers will likely play with radio off for their own VOD safety, which helps clippers.
- Launch-day tracklists mean Content ID reference files for new exclusive tracks may be registered before or at launch; claims could arrive retroactively days later.

### Gaps
- No official confirmation either way on a GTA 6 streamer mode as of 17 November 2026; will be knowable from the settings menu on 18-19 November.
- No Take-Two/Zelnick statement on music licensing terms for creator use was found.
- No hard data on the share of GTA V clips that receive music claims.

## 5. Age-restriction and advertiser-friendliness (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram)

### Takeaway
YouTube has changed its rules twice with GTA 6 in view: from 17 November 2025 it age-restricts gaming videos focused on realistic violence, torture or mass violence against civilians with human-like characters, and in September 2026 it clarified ad rules so that graphic game violence in the first seven seconds or thumbnail gets limited/no ads while later violence can monetise. Ordinary gameplay is ad-eligible; rampage/torture montages are the problem. TikTok and Instagram have no GTA-specific rules found; their general graphic-violence rules restrict recommendation and under-18 distribution.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For Shorts-length clips the "first seven seconds" rule is close to the whole hook: a 20-40 second clip that opens on a graphic kill will be limited-ads on YouTube. Clips need a non-violent or reaction-led opening, with violence, if any, after second eight.
- Age-restricted Shorts lose signed-out and under-18 viewers, a large share of the GTA short-form audience, and are widely understood not to be recommended in the Shorts feed; the reach penalty matters more than the ad penalty for a pay-per-view clipping campaign.
- GTA 6 is rated for mature audiences and features realistic human characters, so it sits exactly inside the November 2025 age-restriction scope when a clip's focus is civilian massacre or torture; ordinary driving, police chases, comedy, glitches and reactions do not.
- Clipping campaigns that pay on TikTok/Instagram views inherit those platforms' quieter suppression (ineligible for recommendation) with no notice to the uploader, which shows up only as low views.

## 6. Who owns what in a clip of a streamer playing GTA 6, and what a third-party clipper needs

### Takeaway
A stream clip is a stack of rights: Take-Two owns the game audiovisuals, music labels/publishers own the radio tracks, and the streamer owns their own performance, commentary, camera feed and broadcast. The person who cuts the clip acquires nothing. A third-party clipper therefore needs the streamer's permission for the stream layer and relies on Rockstar's tolerance (not a licence) for the game layer; neither the streamer nor Rockstar can clear the music.

### Cited Findings
- Twitch's terms treat each streamer as the rights holder of the content they broadcast (Bird & Bird analysis citing clause 10 of Twitch's ToS). — [Bird & Bird, BrandWrites](https://brandwrites.twobirds.com/post/102itse/copyright-of-streamers-on-twitch-and-its-relationship-with-traditional-media)
- Twitch Creator Camp explains that game, music and other third-party content in a stream remain owned by their rights holders and are subject to DMCA. — [Twitch Creator Camp](https://twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/paths/copyrights-and-your-channel/understanding-copyright-law-and-the-dmca-as-a-twitch-streamer)
- The game developer/publisher owns the visual and audio content of the game shown in the video; the streamer owns the recorded performance and production elements. — [legal-tech blog, Dec 2025](https://fauzaniraldi-legaltech.blogspot.com/2025/12/streaming-screenshots-and-gameplay-who.html); [Above the Law, Sept 2018](https://abovethelaw.com/2018/09/a-twitch-in-time-legal-issues-catch-up-with-popular-game-broadcasting-platform/)
- Music carries separate composition and sound-recording rights; publisher streaming permission may not cover third-party music in the game. — [Bristows](https://www.bristows.com/news/video-game-streaming); [Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/twitch-copyright-claims-strikes-and-safe-practices)
- The person who clicks "Clip" does not become the owner of the underlying works; the platform clip tool is a feature, not a copyright licence; embedding/linking differs from downloading, editing and re-uploading a copy to another platform. — [ClipSpeed](https://clipspeed.ai/blog/can-you-clip-someone-elses-stream.html); [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/clipping/clipping-rights-and-permission.md)
- Recommended scope of permission: the exact content, target platforms, whether editing and monetisation are allowed, and duration. — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/clipping/clipping-rights-and-permission.md)
- Precedent of streamers enforcing their layer: a lawyer for Mizkif and other streamers acted against a site selling Twitch clips as NFTs. — [Gfinity](https://gfinity.net/entertainment-news/the-lawyer-for-mizkif-and-other-streamers-takes-on-nft-site-illegally-selling-twitch-clips/)
- Rockstar's policy tolerates only non-commercial fan use and reserves takedown rights at any time (see section 2). — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material)

### Inferences
- An official streamer-run clipping programme (Discord, Whop, Content Rewards and similar) is in effect the streamer's licence for the performance layer; it solves the streamer-side claim but grants nothing on Take-Two's or the labels' side, because the streamer does not hold those rights.
- Clipping a streamer without joining their programme exposes the clipper to takedowns from two directions (streamer and Take-Two); clipping with permission leaves only the Take-Two and music exposure, which is the same exposure the streamer already carries.
- Fair use (US) or quotation/criticism exceptions (elsewhere) are a case-by-case defence, weaker for unedited re-uploads and stronger for transformative, commentary-led edits; they do not stop a platform takedown in the first instance.

### Gaps
- No case law or Take-Two statement specifically on third-party clip channels of GTA streams was found.
- Kick's and YouTube's terms on ownership of broadcasts were not retrieved; only Twitch's position (via Bird & Bird) is cited.
- Most sources on clipper rights are vendor blogs, not legal authorities.
