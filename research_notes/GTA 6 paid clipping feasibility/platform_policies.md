> **[PRESS]** = reputable news coverage (TechCrunch, Tubefilter, Engadget, Dexerto, Digital Trends, Android Authority, PPC Land, Search Engine Journal, eMarketer, Social Media Today);

## 1. YouTube: reused content policy, "inauthentic content" policy, Shorts rules, penalties, copyright, detection evasion

### Takeaway
YouTube runs three separate layers that all bite clip channels: (a) the YPP **reused content** policy (channel-level monetization; requires a "meaningful difference" from the source; explicitly applies even with the original creator's permission and independently of copyright claims); (b) the **inauthentic content** policy (renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 Jul 2025, re-explained in three categories in Jul 2026; targets mass-produced/templated output); and (c) since **1 Oct 2026** a **Shorts recommendation change** that reduces distribution for channels that mainly re-upload other creators' clips without significant changes. Cosmetic alterations made to dodge matching (mirroring, speed/pitch shifts, cropping, heavy filters) are named in the Spam policy as **detection evasion**, a Community Guidelines violation rather than a monetization nuance.

### Cited Findings

**Reused content policy (YPP channel monetization policies)**
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] Definition: reused content "refers to channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value." — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) (surfaced by search, Nov 2026)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] The test: YouTube allows reused content "if viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video." — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] Permission does not cure it: the guideline "applies even if you have permission from the original creator." — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); repeated in [ForkOff reused-content guide 2026](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/clipping/youtube-reused-content-policy-2026) [3P-blog]
- [OFFICIAL-snippet, via secondary quotes] YouTube's own "allowed to monetize" examples include clips used for critical review, reaction videos with commentary, and "edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); quoted in [CreatorWarden](https://creatorwarden.com/guide/reused-content) [3P-blog]
- [OFFICIAL-snippet, via secondary quotes] YouTube's "not allowed" examples include "clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative" and "short videos you compiled from other social media websites." — quoted in [vidIQ reused content guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/) [3P-blog]; official page: [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] The reused-content section lists as violating: "Songs modified to change the pitch or speed, but are otherwise identical to the original song." — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [3P-blog, consistent across several] The policy is assessed on the channel as a whole, not video by video; a violation can remove monetization from the entire channel including long-form. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/); [PanelFollows](https://panelfollows.com/en/blog/youtube-reused-content-policy-shorts-originality-update)
- [3P-blog, consistent] Reused content is separate from copyright: a channel with zero Content ID claims can still fail the reused-content review. — [ChannelTerminated guide](https://channelterminated.com/demonetized/reused-content/); [ForkOff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/clipping/youtube-reused-content-policy-2026)
- [3P-blog] YouTube's policy page names no "safe" clip length. — [ForkOff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/clipping/youtube-reused-content-policy-2026)
- [3P-blog, unverified] One 2026 guide says that since Jul 2026 "reading out other people's material" and pitch-/speed-shifted songs sit inside the reused-content section. — [PanelFollows](https://panelfollows.com/en/blog/youtube-reused-content-policy-shorts-originality-update)

**Inauthentic content policy (ex-"repetitious content")**
- [PRESS] On 15 Jul 2025 YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content"; definition unchanged: content that is mass-produced or repetitive and viewed as spam. — [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-clarifies-july-15-policy-update-repetitious-content-rebranded-as-inauthentic-content/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] YouTube's page states there is "no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) (surfaced via search)
- [PRESS] Rene Ritchie (YouTube Head of Editorial & Creator Liaison) described the Jul 2025 change as a "minor update" aimed at mass-produced/repetitive spam, not a ban on AI, reactions or clips; reaction formats and repurposed content stay eligible where originality/transformative value is evident. — [Engadget, Jul 2025](https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/never-fear-reaction-videos-are-still-allowed-under-youtubes-new-inauthentic-content-policy-222401009.html); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/youtube-issues-clarification-on-monetisation-policy-update-after-creator-backlash-145327.html)
- [PRESS, interpretation] Search Engine Journal notes the clarification does **not** explicitly exempt all reaction or clip channels; channels relying on recycled content without significant added value may still have problems. — [Search Engine Journal, Jul 2025](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)
- [PRESS] Jul 2026 re-explanation: YouTube restated the inauthentic-content guidance as three categories that cannot be monetized — (1) "generic or repetitive content" (looks templated / repetitive across a channel's videos), (2) content that is "unsatisfying or off-putting", (3) content with fake AI "experts" (AI personas posing as human experts on health, legal, financial or political topics). Framed as clarification, not new rules. — [Tubefilter, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)
- [3P-blog] Only the third category names AI; AI-assisted production as such is not stated to be demonetized. — [NoobClaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026/)

**Shorts: monetization eligibility and ineligible views**
- [PRESS] YPP via Shorts: 10 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days; Shorts views and long-form watch hours never combine or cross-count. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-partner-program-eligibility-metrics-for-watch-hours/)
- [3P-blog] Tiers: 500 subscribers for fan funding and Shopping; 1,000 subscribers for ad revenue sharing. — [vidIQ YPP guide 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-update)
- [OFFICIAL wording via multiple secondary sources] Shorts monetization policy excludes views of non-original Shorts: "unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows", "re-uploads of other creators' content from YouTube or other platforms", and "compilations with no original content added." These views are ineligible and not counted when Shorts payments are calculated. — [vidIQ Shorts monetization 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/); [ForkOff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/clipping/youtube-reused-content-policy-2026) (official page: support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220, not fetched)
- [3P-blog] Shorts cut from your own long-form, or carrying your own face/voice commentary, are treated as original. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-original-content-update/)
- [3P-blog, upcoming, unverified] Several guides say YPP ad-revenue thresholds are scheduled to rise on 1 Feb 2027 with existing members grandfathered. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/trending/youtube-updates-its-monetization-rules-what-creators-need-to-know). One blog citing PPC Land says that from 1 Feb 2027 qualified Shorts views counted toward YPP thresholds will exclude re-uploads and compilations without original additions (single source). — [PanelFollows](https://panelfollows.com/en/blog/youtube-reused-content-policy-shorts-originality-update)

**1 Oct 2026 Shorts recommendation change (new, directly targets clip channels)**
- [PRESS] On 1 Oct 2026 YouTube said it is updating Shorts recommendations to prioritize original videos; channels that "mainly aggregate or re-upload other creators' clips without significant changes" will likely get less Shorts-feed distribution. — [Dexerto, ~5 Oct 2026](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-shorts-promoting-original-content-reposts-3718668/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/)
- [PRESS] It is a recommendation (reach) change, not a new monetization policy and not a removal/ban of reused footage; monetization remains governed by the existing reused-content rules. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/phones/youtube-cracks-down-on-reposted-shorts-with-a-new-recommendation-update/); [IBTimes SG](https://www.ibtimes.sg/youtube-targets-recycled-shorts-algorithm-shifts-toward-original-content-94594)
- [PRESS/3P] Creators reusing another creator's clip are encouraged to add "commentary, unique edits, or storytelling." Stated as **not** enough: voice-over that merely narrates what is on screen, minor technical edits, and bulk template changes. Attributed to Rene Ritchie in a Creator Insider video of 1 Oct 2026. — [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/youtubes-shorts-algorithm-now-favors-original-content-over-reposted-clips); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/youtube/youtube-shorts-original-content-reach-update/)
- [PRESS] Judgment is at channel level; YouTube gave no ratio, lookback window or definition of "significant", and no precise rollout date. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/)
- [PRESS] Ritchie suggested sharing other people's videos via the Posts tab instead of re-uploading them as Shorts. — [tbreak](https://tbreak.com/youtube-shorts-clipping-original-content/)
- [3P-blog] A longer third-party list of non-qualifying edits adds crops, flips, speed changes, filters and transcribed captions — this is not confirmed as official YouTube wording. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-original-content-update/)

**Consequences on review**
- [3P/PRESS] Application rejection or removal: appeal within 21 days, or re-apply after 30 days; after a second rejection the re-apply wait is 90 days (rule in force since 5 Jun 2023 per one transcript-based source). — [vidIQ YPP guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-update); [Tella](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-join-youtube-partner-program)
- [3P-blog] Making videos private or deleting them during a review "might impact the status of your application." — [vidIQ YPP guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-update)

**Copyright: Content ID claims vs strikes**
- [3P, consistent] A Content ID claim attaches to a single video; the claimant can monetize, track or block it; it is not a strike and does not by itself penalize the channel. — [vidIQ claim vs strike](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-copyright-claim-copyright-strike/); [RouteNote](https://support.routenote.com/?p=5994)
- [3P] A copyright strike follows a legal removal request YouTube deems valid, and applies to the whole channel; a disputed claim can escalate to a removal request and thus a strike. — [Trademark Engine 2026](https://www.trademarkengine.com/blog/youtube-copyright-claims-vs-copyright-strikes-creators-need-to-know/); [copyrighted.com](https://www.copyrighted.com/blog/copyright-claim-vs-strike)
- [3P] Three strikes within 90 days put a channel at risk of termination. — [ChannelTerminated](https://channelterminated.com/demonetized/reused-content/)
- [3P] Rights holders can file a manual claim/takedown when automated matching misses. — [dandi.media](https://www.dandi.media/en/seven-second-rule/)

**Detection evasion**
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] YouTube Spam policy, "Detection evasion: Technical manipulation (such as speeding up audio, heavy filters, cropping) designed to bypass abuse detection." Official example: "Uploading a television episode that has been mirrored horizontally to avoid detection." — [YouTube Help: Spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] Same policy bars "Scraped content: Re-posting material from other websites or platforms, or other videos without adding anything of your own", and mass-produced content with minimal changes. — [YouTube Help: Spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)
- [3P] Spam/deceptive-practices enforcement is a Community Guidelines matter that can lead to channel loss; one guide says spam terminations often skip the warning/strike ladder (not confirmed on an official page). — [ChannelTerminated spam guide](https://channelterminated.com/reasons/spam-deceptive-practices/)
- [3P, legal commentary] Altering pitch, speed, framing or metadata merely to avoid detection "may create additional enforcement risk." — [Ahlawat Law](https://ahlawat.law/account-terminated-the-legal-realities-of-youtubes-circumvention-policy-and-compliant-paths-to-recovery/)

### Inferences
- A paid clipper posting lightly edited cuts of someone else's stream fails on YouTube at three independent points: Shorts views are "ineligible" for the revenue pool, the channel is exposed to a reused-content YPP rejection/removal, and (since Oct 2026) the channel's Shorts reach is throttled. None of these depends on a copyright complaint, and none is cured by the streamer's permission.
- The practical bar YouTube describes is authorship that a viewer can perceive: the clipper's own commentary, analysis, storyline or substantive editing. Edits that only re-package the same footage are on the wrong side of the line.
- The Jul 2026 "generic or repetitive / templated" category is a second, separate hazard for clip farms that push many near-identical videos through one template, even where each clip is individually trimmed.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the official pages, so the complete official "allowed / not allowed" example lists and the exact Shorts-policy sentence are not verified verbatim. [RECALL-unverified] The official reused-content page, as the researcher remembers it, also lists as allowed: "a scene from a movie where you've rewritten the dialogue and changed the voiceover", "replays of a sports tournament where you explain what a competitor did to succeed"; and as not allowed: "content uploaded many times by other creators", "promotion of other people's content (even if you have permission)", "content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications". It also states that reused content is "separate from YouTube's copyright enforcement, which means it's not based on copyright, permission, or fair use." Verify at support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392.
- [RECALL-unverified] Shorts revenue share (creators keep 45% of allocated Shorts revenue) and the expanded-tier Shorts threshold (500 subs + 3M Shorts views in 90 days) were not confirmed this session.
- No primary YouTube post for the 1 Oct 2026 Shorts change was reached; Ritchie's wording is second-hand.
- The reported 1 Feb 2027 threshold changes are from third-party blogs only.
- No official statement found on whether YouTube treats clips from an *authorised paid clipping campaign* differently in the Shorts recommendation system.

## 2. TikTok: unoriginal/reproduced content, For You feed eligibility, Creator Rewards Program, penalties

### Takeaway
TikTok does not usually remove unedited re-uploads; it makes them **ineligible for the For You feed** and **ineligible for monetization** (Creator Rewards Program uses originality as a formula metric). CRP also structurally excludes typical short clips because qualifying videos must be original and at least one minute long, on a 10k-follower/100k-view account.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] For You feed Eligibility Standards list as ineligible: "reproduced or unoriginal content that is imported or uploaded without any new or creative edits, such as content with someone else's visible watermark or superimposed logo." — [TikTok FYF Eligibility Standards (2024H1 snapshot)](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards?cgversion=2024H1update)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] TikTok Creator Academy defines unoriginal content four ways: copied content; content largely repurposed without creative edits; content combined from multiple sources with little added value; content carrying another party's visible watermark or logo. — [TikTok Creator Academy: originality policy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/tiktok-originality-policy)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] "Unoriginal content may be removed from the For You feed, making it harder to discover"; it is "also ineligible for TikTok's monetization programs like the Creator Rewards Program, which uses 'originality' as a key metric in its rewards formula." — [TikTok Creator Academy: originality policy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/tiktok-originality-policy)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] FYF ineligibility shows in the TikTok analytics tool and can be appealed. — [TikTok Creator Academy: integrity & authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/integrity-authenticity-on-tiktok)
- [3P summary of Creator Academy] Examples given as insufficient: adding subtitles to someone else's audio; basic text summaries over another person's clip; "only splicing clips together." — [ShapelessAI notes](https://shapelessai.com/notes/unoriginal-content-rules-youtube-tiktok-instagram)
- [3P-blog] Light changes cited as not creating originality: speed adjustments, filters, fixed text, stickers. — [InfluenceFlow](https://influenceflow.io/resources/why-is-my-tiktok-unoriginal-content-flag-appearing-and-how-to-fix-it/)
- [3P-blog] The originality/watermark text is the same in the Sept 2026 Community Guidelines as in the 2025 version; new guidelines published 25 Aug 2026, effective 24 Sept 2026 (date not verified on TikTok's site). — [FastClips](https://fastclips.app/learn/tiktok-community-guidelines-september-2026); [Fastlane](https://www.usefastlane.ai/blog/tiktok-community-guidelines)
- [3P, consistent across many 2026 guides] Creator Rewards Program account requirements: 18+ (19+ South Korea), 10,000+ followers, 100,000+ video views in last 30 days, personal account in good standing, eligible country. — [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-join-the-tiktok-creator-rewards-program); [Gemlist](https://www.gemlist.io/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-requirements); [PostLink](https://postlinkapp.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program)
- [3P citing CRP Terms (US)] Video requirements: at least 1 minute long; original; pays on "qualified views"; excludes Duets, Stitches, Photo Mode posts and sponsored/paid promotional content. — [Gemlist](https://www.gemlist.io/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-requirements); [MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-how-organic-monetization-works-in-2026)
- [3P] Country list conflicts between sources; one gives eight countries as of 15 Sept 2026 (US, UK, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico, Brazil). — [Viewlify](https://viewlify.app/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-requirements)
- [3P] Penalty path described for identical reposts: suppressed distribution escalating to bans for persistent low-originality posting; gaming CRP "can pull you out of the program entirely." — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/clipping-on-tiktok); [MakeViral](https://www.makeviral.ai/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program)
- [3P, anecdotal] Creators report being flagged "unoriginal" for their own content, with appeals hard to pursue. — [Baller Alert](https://balleralert.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-disqualification/)
- [PRESS] TikTok (and Instagram) are cracking down on clipping campaigns that do not clearly tell viewers the account runner was paid, hiding those posts from main feeds. — [eMarketer, ~Jun 2026](https://www.emarketer.com/content/clipping-tests-limits-social-media-authenticity)
- [3P] 2026 guideline text on undisclosed commercial content: "we may reduce its visibility or apply the content disclosure setting." TikTok's Jan 2026 help page says posts promoting a brand, product or service must turn on the commercial-content disclosure setting. — [FastClips](https://fastclips.app/learn/tiktok-community-guidelines-september-2026); [Posthype](https://www.posthype.news/article/clipping-ftc-disclosure)

### Inferences
- Sponsored/paid promotional content is excluded from CRP, so a clip posted under a paid clipping campaign is doubly outside TikTok's own payout: it is promotional content and (usually) unoriginal and under 60 seconds. On TikTok, the clipper's only income is the campaign payout.
- The reach risk on TikTok is FYF ineligibility rather than removal: a clip that is judged unoriginal stays up but earns few views, which directly undermines a pay-per-view clipping model.

### Gaps
- Official CRP terms and the current (Sept 2026) Community Guidelines could not be opened; thresholds are from third-party guides (consistent with each other).
- Appeal success rates and "three violations = permanent monetization loss" claims found on blogs are unverified and omitted.
- No dedicated TikTok announcement about clip farms was found; no official text located on many accounts posting the same clip (see section 5).

## 3. Instagram and Facebook (Meta): originality rules, monetization, "meaningful enhancement"

### Takeaway
Meta has moved in steps from de-ranking reposts (2022) to replacing them with originals and delisting aggregators from Reels recommendations (Apr 2024), to monetization and reach penalties on Facebook (Jul 2025), to excluding unoriginal-majority Instagram accounts from all recommendation surfaces across Reels, photos and carousels (30 Apr 2026). Meta's stated bar is "meaningful enhancements" — commentary, parody, creative editing, reaction, voice-over that adds value — and it says watermarks, borders, basic captions and speed changes do not count.

### Cited Findings

**Instagram**
- [PRESS] Apr 2022: Adam Mosseri announced ranking would favour originality and be less likely to recommend reposts or accounts that aggregate others' content, saying Instagram should not overvalue aggregators. — [TechCrunch, 20 Apr 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/20/instagram-ranking-system-recommend-original-content/)
- [PRESS] 30 Apr 2024: Instagram began replacing reposted Reels with the original in recommendations when it finds duplicates, and stopped recommending Reels from aggregator accounts that shared unoriginal content "10 or more times" in the previous 30 days. — [Nasdaq/RTTNews](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/instagram-updates-its-reels-algorithm-to-prioritize-original-content); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-algorithm-to-benefit-original-creators/819016/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414773/instagram-algorithm-update-discourages-reposted-co.html). (Tom's Guide words the threshold as "more than 10 times in a month" — [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/computing/social-media/your-instagram-video-feed-is-going-to-be-better-soon-heres-how).)
- [PRESS] 30 Apr 2026: accounts that regularly repost content they did not create, or primarily share others' work, as photos and carousels (in addition to Reels) are no longer eligible for recommendations anywhere in the app (feed recommendations, Explore/Discover). Followers still see the posts. — [TechCrunch, 30 Apr 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/)
- [PRESS] Per TechCrunch, "low-effort edits, such as adding watermarks or changing the speed of a video" do not count as original; nor does a screenshot of someone's post with the username visible as credit. — [TechCrunch, 30 Apr 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/)
- [3P quoting Instagram] "If your account is not eligible to be recommended, none of your content will be recommended." Eligibility returns when most posts in a rolling 30-day period are original. — [Status 200 Uploads](https://status200uploads.com/blog/instagram-originality-rules-reposts); [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/instagram-restricts-reach-content-aggregators/)
- [3P] Counted as original: genuine commentary, creative edits, educational overlays, humour, voiceover, green-screen reactions adding value beyond restating the source. Not counted: border, watermark or basic caption only. — [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/instagram-restricts-reach-content-aggregators/); [Mark Studios](https://www.markstudios.com/blog/instagram-original-content-crackdown-2026)
- [3P] Reposted content gets a label linking to the original creator. — [ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/instagram-original-content-algorithm-update/)
- [3P] Sept 2026 Mosseri ranking explainer series names originality as one of four ranking principles. — [Bazzaal](https://www.bazzaal.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-mosseri)
- [PRESS] Instagram "has completely blocked unoriginal content from its recommendation system", targeting accounts that re-upload footage without adding anything new or creative. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/clipping-tests-limits-social-media-authenticity)

**Facebook**
- [PRESS] 24 Apr 2025: Facebook announced reach and monetization cuts for spammy content. — [TechCrunch, 24 Apr 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/24/facebook-cracks-down-on-spammy-content-by-cutting-reach-and-monetization/)
- [PRESS] 14 Jul 2025: Meta announced that accounts which "improperly reuse someone else's videos, photos or text posts repeatedly" lose access to Facebook monetization programs for a period and get reduced distribution on everything they share. — [TechCrunch, 14 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content); [Tubefilter, 15 Jul 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/)
- [PRESS] Duplicate videos: Facebook reduces distribution of detected copies so the original gets the reach, and is testing links on duplicates pointing viewers to the original. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/meta-follows-youtube-in-crackdown-on-unoriginal-content/551096/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/facebook-introduces-stronger-measures-against-unoriginal-content-creators/)
- [PRESS] Meta said the change should not affect creators who add their own take: reaction videos, trend participation and voice-over narration remain acceptable provided the contribution adds real value; target is repeated reposting "without permission or meaningful enhancements." — [GIGAZINE, 15 Jul 2025](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250715-meta-combating-unoriginal-facebook-content/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/facebook-introduces-stronger-measures-against-unoriginal-content-creators/)
- [PRESS] Meta's creator guidance: post original material, make meaningful additions when using others' content, avoid third-party watermarks. — [MediaNama, Jul 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-meta-cracks-down-on-unoriginal-facebook-content/)
- [PRESS] Scale: ~500,000 accounts actioned for spammy behaviour/fake engagement in H1 2025 (reduced distribution through to loss of monetization) and ~10 million profiles impersonating large creators removed; rollout gradual over following months. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/meta-platforms-crack-down-unoriginal-045034281.html)
- [3P quoting Meta guidance] Content "reproduced without making meaningful enhancements (commentary, parody, creative editing, etc.) cannot be monetized." — [Creators Agency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/facebook-content-monetization-requirements-2026)
- [3P] Facebook Content Monetization is invite-only (Help Center wording as of 18 Oct 2026: "Facebook Content Monetization is currently invite-only"); Meta publishes no universal follower/view threshold; third-party thresholds conflict (5,000 vs 10,000). — [MonetizedYet](https://monetizedyet.com/facebook-monetization-requirements/); [Creators Agency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/facebook-content-monetization-requirements-2026)
- [3P] "Limited originality of content" is one of the most common monetization restriction flags; loss normally follows repeated violations; appeals via Professional Dashboard. Permission to repost does not by itself make content eligible. — [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/facebook-ai-content-monetization-policy); [Clip-Short](https://www.clip-short.com/guides/facebook-content-monetization)

### Inferences
- On Instagram the unit of punishment is the account: once most of a 30-day window is unoriginal, nothing from the account is recommended to non-followers. A dedicated clip account with few followers therefore gets close to zero reach, which is fatal for pay-per-view clipping.
- Because Meta swaps duplicates for the original and links back to it, the source creator may still benefit from clipper activity even while the clipper's copy is suppressed.
- Meta's Jul 2025 wording ("without permission or meaningful enhancements") suggests permission may be relevant to the Facebook penalty, but its monetization guidance independently requires meaningful enhancement, so permission alone probably does not unlock monetization.

### Gaps
- Meta's primary posts (Instagram blog 30 Apr 2024; Facebook creators blog 14 Jul 2025; Instagram Apr 2026 announcement) were not opened; exact wording is via press. The widely repeated phrase about not just "stitching together clips or adding your watermark" could not be confirmed in any search extract.
- No reliable source found on Instagram-specific monetization programme eligibility for clip accounts in 2026.
- Reach figures on blogs ("40–60% more distribution", "60–80% drops") are unverified and should not be used.
- Whether Instagram's originality classifier treats a clip posted with the original creator's permission (or via an official Collab post) as original is not addressed in any source found.

## 4. The clipper "editing rules": genuine value-add, myth, or prohibited evasion?

### Takeaway
Platforms recognise only edits that add the uploader's own perceivable contribution (commentary, analysis, storyline, substantive creative editing). Cosmetic re-packaging (captions, borders, crops, speed changes, watermarks, templates) is repeatedly listed as insufficient, numeric "rules" (N seconds, 30% changed) have no basis in policy or law, and mirroring/speed/pitch/crop tricks applied to dodge matching are named by YouTube as detection evasion.

### Cited Findings (per "rule")
| "Rule" | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Voiceover / commentary | **Genuine value-add if it is real commentary**; not if it only describes the screen | YouTube: commentary and reaction listed as allowed; narration of what is on screen does not qualify — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [PPC Land, Oct 2026](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/). - Do the evasion tricks work? Rights holders can always file manual claims/takedowns when automation misses — [dandi.media](https://www.dandi.media/en/seven-second-rule/). Short excerpts can still draw Content ID claims — [LegalHelp](https://legalhelp.us/how-much-of-a-song-can-you-use-on-youtube-fair-use-and-legal-risk/).

### Inferences
- The platforms' originality systems (YouTube reused-content review and the Oct 2026 Shorts ranking; Instagram's originality classifier; TikTok's originality metric) evaluate whether the uploader contributed something, not whether a fingerprint matcher fires. So even where a cosmetic trick did defeat automated copyright matching, it would not make the clip "original" for monetization or recommendation purposes, and on YouTube it converts a monetization problem into a Community Guidelines violation.
- Split-screen-with-gameplay, a text hook and auto-captions are best read as packaging: they sit in the same class as "minor technical edits" and "template changes" that YouTube and Instagram say do not qualify, though no platform names split-screen specifically.
- The standard clipper toolkit (auto-captions + vertical reframe + hook text) is therefore not a violation but is also not what any platform says makes a clip monetizable or recommendable.

### Gaps
- No credible, current evidence was found on how reliably mirroring/speed/pitch changes defeat Content ID or Meta/TikTok matching; no claim is made either way beyond "it is a named policy violation on YouTube and manual claims remain possible."
- No official wording found from any platform on split-screen gameplay formats.
- The detailed list attributed to YouTube's Oct 2026 update (crops, flips, speed, filters, transcribed captions) is a third-party list; only "narration of what's on screen, minor edits, template changes" is attributed to Ritchie, and that second-hand.

## 5. Does the original creator's permission (paid clipping campaign) change treatment? How are many accounts posting the same clip treated?

### Takeaway
Permission resolves the copyright exposure but not the originality problem: YouTube says its reused-content rule applies even with permission, and Meta's monetization guidance requires meaningful enhancement regardless. Mass duplication is specifically disfavoured: Meta and Instagram demote duplicates in favour of the original, YouTube's spam policy covers mass-produced/scraped content, and paid clips also trigger paid-promotion disclosure duties whose breach can itself reduce reach.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] YouTube: the reused-content guideline "applies even if you have permission from the original creator." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [3P] Meta: you can have permission to repost something that still contributes too little original value to be monetized. — [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/facebook-ai-content-monetization-policy)
- [PRESS] Facebook's Jul 2025 penalty is framed around repeated reposting "without permission or meaningful change." — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250715-meta-combating-unoriginal-facebook-content/)
- [PRESS] Facebook reduces distribution of duplicate videos and tests links to the original; Instagram replaces reposts with the original in recommendations. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/meta-follows-youtube-in-crackdown-on-unoriginal-content/551096/); [Nasdaq/RTTNews](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/instagram-updates-its-reels-algorithm-to-prioritize-original-content)
- [OFFICIAL-snippet] YouTube Spam policy covers mass-produced content with minimal changes and scraped re-posts "without adding anything of your own." — [YouTube Spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)
- [3P] TikTok: multiple accounts are allowed, but not to deceive or break rules; deceptive account behaviour can lead to restrictions or bans. — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/clipping-on-tiktok)
- [3P] Campaign-side rules: Whop Content Rewards terms prohibit duplicate deliverables and require each submission to be unique; clips co-posted with other accounts may be rejected; bot/script views excluded. — [Clyper](https://www.clyper.io/guides/whop-clipping); [Treza Labs](https://www.trezalabs.com/blog/why-clips-get-rejected-on-clipping-campaigns)
- [3P] Campaign payout and platform payout are separate questions: a campaign pays by its own rules regardless of whether YouTube/TikTok monetizes the account. — [Viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/guides/clipping-campaigns)
- [PRESS] TikTok and Instagram are hiding from main feeds clipping-campaign posts that don't clearly disclose the account was paid; disguised-ad clipping carries higher legal risk than promoting entertainment/podcasts. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/clipping-tests-limits-social-media-authenticity)
- [3P] Each platform has a paid-content switch (TikTok commercial content toggle, Instagram paid partnership label, YouTube paid promotion setting); FTC guidance requires clear disclosure of the paid relationship. — [Posthype](https://www.posthype.news/article/clipping-ftc-disclosure); [FindClout](https://findclout.com/blog/is-clipping-legal)
- [PRESS] YouTube itself is building first-party clipping for owners: YouTube Studio's clips tool now turns 16:9 podcast segments into vertical Shorts (Nov 2026). — [PPC Land, 12 Nov 2026](https://ppc.land/youtube-studio-now-turns-16-9-podcast-segments-into-vertical-shorts/)

### Inferences
- A paid clipping campaign changes who can complain (the rights holder won't strike its own clippers) but leaves every originality-based mechanism in place: no Shorts revenue share on YouTube, no CRP on TikTok, likely "limited originality" on Facebook, and recommendation suppression on Instagram/YouTube Shorts for accounts that mostly repost.
- Many accounts posting the same clip is the pattern the 2024–2026 changes were designed against; on Meta surfaces the likely outcome is that the original (or earliest) upload is surfaced and copies are demoted.
- Paid clips are commercial content; non-disclosure adds a second, independent reason for reach reduction on TikTok/Instagram and a regulatory exposure.

### Gaps
- No official platform statement was found that explicitly addresses authorised paid clipping campaigns (as distinct from generic reposting) in recommendations.
- No official TikTok or YouTube text located that quantifies treatment of the same clip across many accounts; third-party descriptions only.
- Whether the Oct 2026 YouTube Shorts change exempts creators/brands clipping their own long-form "went unaddressed" per [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/).

## 6. AI-generated / altered content disclosure relevant to auto-captions, AI voiceover, AI titles

### Takeaway
Disclosure duties on all four platforms hinge on *realistic* synthetic or significantly altered media; productivity uses such as automatic captions, scripts and idea/title generation are not disclosure triggers on YouTube. AI voiceover is a grey area (required where it imitates a real person; unclear for a generic synthetic narrator). Separately, AI use does not rescue originality: templated AI narration over others' clips falls into the "generic or repetitive" inauthentic-content category.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube requires creators to disclose realistic content made with altered or synthetic media; disclosure is not required for AI used for productivity such as generating scripts, content ideas or automatic captions, nor for clearly unrealistic/animated content or minor edits such as colour adjustment and beauty filters. — [YouTube Blog: disclosing altered or synthetic content (Mar 2024)](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)
- [3P] A voice imitating a real person needs labelling; a generic synthetic voice may not. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-inauthentic-content/). YouTube has not published a clear example for a generic synthetic narrator. — [NoFaceTube](https://nofacetube.com/rules/youtube-altered-synthetic-content-disclosure/)
- [PRESS] YouTube (Jul 2025): using AI is fine if content is authentic; the inauthentic-content rule targets mass-produced, repetitive output, not AI as such. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/)
- [PRESS] Jul 2026 category 3: AI personas presented as human experts on health, legal, financial or political topics cannot be monetized. — [Tubefilter, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)
- [3P] TikTok requires a label on realistic AI-generated or significantly edited content (its AIGC setting, or a clear caption/sticker/watermark) and may auto-label when it detects C2PA Content Credentials. — [Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/blog/tiktok-ai-generated-content-label); [Eminence](https://eminencegrowthsolutions.com/tools/emtrace/platforms/tiktok/)
- [3P] AI-generated captions are described as exempt from TikTok labelling (no official source located). — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/)
- [3P] Meta: for organic posts the Help Center reportedly may require a label for photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio (not for images); "AI info" may be applied automatically from industry signals such as C2PA; penalties may apply where a required label is missing. — [SignUnder](https://signunder.com/labels/meta)

### Inferences
- A clip of a real stream with auto-generated captions and an AI-suggested title does not trigger AI disclosure on YouTube; an AI voiceover narrating the clip probably does not either unless it clones a real person's voice, but that same AI narration is unlikely to count as "commentary" for originality if it only describes what is on screen.
- Editing or re-voicing a real streamer so they appear to say something they did not would be realistic altered content requiring disclosure on all platforms.

### Gaps
- TikTok and Meta official AI-labelling pages were not opened; statements are from vendor blogs.
- No official example found on any platform for a generic AI narrator over third-party footage.
- Conflicting third-party claims about Meta ad-disclosure mandates in 2026 were set aside as unreliable and are out of scope for organic clips.
